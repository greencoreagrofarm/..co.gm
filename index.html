🌱 Welcome to GreenCore Agro Farm

GreenCore Agro Farm is an integrated agribusiness initiative based in Sinchu Njengundi, Jarra East District, Lower River Region (LRR), The Gambia. Our four core areas are agroforestry, vegetable production, poultry farming, and animal husbandry. We aim to produce quality food, develop productive tree crops, create rural jobs, and demonstrate responsible, climate-smart farming.

Our integrated model combines short-cycle activities, such as vegetables and poultry, with longer-term investments in fruit trees and managed livestock. This diversified approach can spread market and seasonal risks while building productive farm assets. Water efficiency, soil care, animal welfare, biosecurity, safe food handling, and sound financial records are central to our operating approach.

Our tagline: Growing Food. Restoring Land. Building Prosperity.

📖 About Us

GreenCore Agro Farm is being developed as a diversified farm enterprise serving Sinchu Njengundi and the wider Lower River Region. The business will combine practical food production with long-term land stewardship and a commercial approach to farm management. Farm activities will be developed in phases, according to available land, reliable water access, market demand, technical capacity, and financing.

Vision: To become a trusted model of sustainable, commercially viable, climate-smart integrated farming in The Gambia.

Mission: To produce quality food, cultivate productive trees, raise healthy livestock, create dignified rural employment, and manage land and water resources responsibly.

Core Values: Sustainability, food safety, animal welfare, innovation, transparency, accountability, local employment, and commercial discipline.

Business Focus: Agroforestry and fruit trees; vegetable production; poultry; and animal husbandry, supported by efficient irrigation, appropriate infrastructure, good recordkeeping, and strong customer relationships.

⚠️ The Challenge We Address

Small-scale agricultural enterprises in The Gambia face several connected challenges that GreenCore Agro Farm intends to address through practical, market-oriented farming:

  • Seasonal food supply: Vegetable production and availability can fluctuate with rainfall, water access, and seasonal growing conditions, affecting supply and prices.
  • Water and climate pressures: Reliable, affordable water is essential for dry-season production, young fruit trees, and livestock. Efficient irrigation and water planning are therefore key priorities.
  • Limited diversification: Farms that depend on one crop or product can be vulnerable to price changes, pests, disease, and weather shocks.
  • Post-harvest losses and market access: Poor timing, handling, storage, transport, and limited buyer relationships can reduce farmers' earnings.
  • Productive land and soil management: Long-term farm success depends on protecting soil, planting suitable trees, managing organic matter, and preventing damage from fire, drought, and livestock.
  • Skills and employment: Rural communities need practical opportunities for agricultural training, enterprise development, and decent local employment.

GreenCore's integrated model will respond through diversified production, planned irrigation, improved farm management, market validation, responsible livestock care, and measurable environmental and social outcomes.

💡 Our Integrated Farming Approach

  • Agroforestry: Establish and maintain suitable fruit trees, including grafted oranges, mangoes, cashew, and other locally appropriate species after confirming soil, spacing, water, and market suitability. Trees will be treated as long-term assets, not immediate sources of mature-orchard income.
  • Vegetable production: Grow market-oriented crops such as hot pepper, okra, onion, tomato, cucumber, cabbage, and leafy vegetables according to season, water capacity, buyer demand, and production economics.
  • Poultry farming: Start with a manageable poultry production system, potentially broilers where market demand is verified. Each batch will have a cost plan, hygiene procedures, veterinary guidance, and sales arrangements.
  • Animal husbandry: Consider goats and sheep as the initial livestock focus, with herd size based on feed availability, water, shelter, veterinary care, land capacity, and capital. Expansion into other livestock will follow feasibility assessment.
  • Resource integration: Use composted plant residues and properly treated manure where safe and appropriate, while separating poultry and livestock areas from clean water sources, crop handling, and nursery operations.
  • Climate-smart practices: Prioritise water-efficient irrigation, mulching, soil improvement, safe waste management, fire prevention, and monitoring of production results.

Implementation will be phased. GreenCore will expand activities only when demand, technical capacity, available working capital, and actual production records support the next stage.

📊 Market Opportunity

Potential Customers:

  • Vegetables and fruit: Households, produce traders, market vendors, retailers, restaurants, caterers, and institutional buyers in nearby communities and markets, including Soma where commercially practical.
  • Poultry: Households, food businesses, retailers, poultry traders, and other buyers seeking healthy birds at agreed weights and delivery times.
  • Livestock: Livestock traders, households, butchers, and buyers whose requirements match the farm's animal health and production capacity.
  • Nursery and tree products: Farmers, home gardeners, and community projects seeking healthy, correctly identified seedlings, if a dependable nursery business is established.

Competitive Advantage: GreenCore's combination of vegetables, poultry, livestock, and tree crops offers multiple potential revenue streams. A focus on consistent quality, responsible production, reliable delivery, transparent weighing, and customer relationships can help the business earn repeat sales.

Market Validation: Before investing in large production volumes, the farm will interview prospective buyers, record seasonal prices and preferred product specifications, compare transport and packaging costs, and seek written expressions of interest where possible. Sales projections will be based on verified demand rather than assumptions about export markets.

Sales Channels: Direct farm sales, local traders, pre-orders, WhatsApp Business, social media, and repeat supply arrangements with food businesses and retailers.

⚙️ Operations & Production

Agroforestry and Nursery

GreenCore will assess the site, confirm available planting area, choose suitable varieties, source healthy planting material, and establish a planting and maintenance calendar. Potential crops include oranges, mangoes, cashew, and other locally suitable trees. A preliminary target such as 100 grafted orange trees will be confirmed only after checking spacing, water availability, site layout, and budget.

Vegetable Production

Initial vegetable crops may include hot pepper, okra, onion, tomato, cucumber, cabbage, and leafy vegetables. Production will be planned by season and buyer demand. Drip irrigation may be used where technically and financially suitable. Harvest records will distinguish total harvest from marketable produce actually sold, with losses and input costs tracked by crop.

Poultry Farming

The farm will begin at a scale supported by housing, working capital, feed supply, clean water, and confirmed market demand. Each poultry cycle will track chick purchases, feed, mortality, veterinary costs, weight, labour, and sales. A qualified veterinary professional will guide vaccination and disease prevention. Poultry will be kept separate from livestock pens, crop handling, and clean water facilities.

Animal Husbandry

Goats and sheep may form the initial livestock unit, subject to a site and feed assessment. The farm will provide secure shelter, clean water, suitable feed, veterinary care, and records of animal identification, births, treatments, deaths, breeding, and sales. Herd size will be limited by dry-season feed and water capacity.

Shared Infrastructure and Workflow

Priority infrastructure may include secure fencing, a reliable water source and storage, irrigation, nursery and orchard blocks, vegetable plots, poultry housing, livestock pens, a feed and equipment store, a composting area, and a simple produce sorting and packing space. Final placement will follow a measured site layout that reduces contamination, drainage, fire, and disease risks.

Operating Cycle: Market planning → site and input preparation → planting or stocking → daily crop and animal care → harvest or livestock finishing → grading and safe handling → sales and delivery → financial and production review.

👥 Management & Staffing

Farm Director / Proprietor: Leads strategy, budgeting, partnerships, procurement approval, customer relationships, and reporting to funders or investors.

Farm Operations Supervisor: Coordinates daily work plans, labour, production calendars, inventory, and quality control.

Horticulture and Nursery Lead: Oversees vegetable plots, irrigation, tree establishment, nursery management, crop health, and harvest records.

Poultry and Livestock Attendants: Manage feeding, watering, hygiene, animal observation, housing, and production records under appropriate technical guidance.

Sales and Administration Support: Maintains customer records, orders, receipts, inventory, bookkeeping, and delivery coordination.

Staffing Plan: Staffing will grow in line with production volume and cash flow. During the start-up stage, some roles may be combined or part-time. GreenCore aims to create local employment and practical learning opportunities for youth and women as the business expands. Staff numbers will be set after the operating scale and budget are confirmed.

🚀 Development Roadmap: 2026–2030

Phase 1 — Months 1–3: Confirm land-use rights, measure the site, assess water availability, consult agricultural and veterinary professionals, obtain supplier quotations, interview potential buyers, and complete the farm layout and detailed budget.

Phase 2 — Months 4–6: Develop priority fencing and water infrastructure, prepare vegetable plots, establish a nursery and initial tree blocks, and prepare poultry and livestock facilities with appropriate hygiene and biosecurity measures.

Phase 3 — Months 7–12: Run controlled production cycles, establish repeat customer relationships, maintain production and sales records, review unit costs, and report transparently on employment and environmental indicators.

Year 2: Improve crop yields, tree survival, animal health, production efficiency, and gross margins. Expand only those activities with demonstrated demand and viable unit economics.

Years 3–5: Scale profitable business units, strengthen storage and packaging where commercially justified, expand reliable buyer relationships, and assess nursery sales or other value-added activities only after feasibility review.

Key Performance Indicators: Sales by business unit, gross margin, cash flow, marketable vegetable yield, tree survival, poultry mortality and sale weights, livestock health and sales, repeat customers, jobs created, training delivered, and water-use efficiency where measurable.
